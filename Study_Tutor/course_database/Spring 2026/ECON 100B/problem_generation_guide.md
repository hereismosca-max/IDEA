### Mathematical Toolkit (Very High Confidence)

**From study guide and cheat sheets, these tools appear repeatedly:**

1. **Growth rate rules (CRITICAL):**
   - If z = x/y, then g(z) = g(x) - g(y)
   - If z = xy, then g(z) = g(x) + g(y)
   - If z = x^a, then g(z) = a·g(x)

2. **Compound growth:** y_t = y_0(1 + g)^t

3. **Average growth calculation:** ḡ = (y_t/y_0)^(1/t) - 1

4. **Rule of 70:** Doubling time ≈ 70/g (when g is in percentage points)

5. **GDP accounting identities:**
   - Y = C + I + G + NX
   - S = I (closed economy, no government)
   - S - I = NX (open economy)

### Topic Coverage by Model

**Solow Model (Chapters 4-8, Classes 1-11):**
- Production function: Y = Ā·K^(1/3)·L^(2/3)
- Capital accumulation: Δk = sy - (n+d)k
- Steady state: k* = (sĀ/(n+d))^(3/2)
- Comparative statics: changes in s, d, n, Ā, K destruction, immigration
- Transition dynamics and convergence
- Golden Rule: maximize consumption when MPK = n + d
- **Key insight tested:** Level effects vs. growth effects; only TFP (Ā) changes generate sustained growth

**Romer Model (Chapters 9-11):**
- Idea production: g[A] = z̄·ℓ·N
- Output: y_t = A_0(1-ℓ)(1+g[A])^t
- Nonrival nature of ideas
- Comparative statics: changes in N, ℓ, z̄
- **Key insight tested:** Population size matters for growth; ideas are more important than capital

**Short-Run Model (Second half, PS4 + Final Review):**
- IS-MP-PC framework
- Financial crises and Great Recession
- Monetary policy (Federal Reserve, interest rates)
- Fiscal policy effects
- Inflation dynamics
- **From PS4:** Government spending, investment, saving relationships

## Main Topics To Test

### Core Long-Run Growth (Midterm Focus)

1. **GDP Accounting & Measurement**
   - Expenditure approach components (C, I, G, NX) and typical US shares
   - Real vs. nominal GDP, deflation by price level
   - What investment creates (physical capital)
   - Definition of capital in growth models

2. **Growth Mathematics**
   - Apply three growth rate rules
   - Compute compound growth over multiple periods
   - Calculate average growth rates
   - Use Rule of 70
   - Relate real and nominal growth via inflation

3. **Production Functions**
   - Cobb-Douglas properties: Y = ĀK^(1/3)L^(2/3)
   - Constant returns to scale
   - Diminishing marginal products
   - MPK and MPL conditions for profit maximization
   - Per-worker form: y = Āk^(1/3)

4. **Solow Model Mechanics**
   - Derive steady state algebraically
   - Graph capital accumulation diagram (k on x-axis)
   - Identify sy curve and (n+d)k line
   - Comparative statics for all parameters
   - Transition dynamics and convergence speed
   - Distinguish level effects from growth effects
   - Golden Rule consumption maximization

5. **Romer Model Mechanics**
   - Derive growth rate of ideas: g[A] = z̄ℓN
   - Compute output path: y_t = A_0(1-ℓ)(1+g[A])^t
   - Graph on ratio scale (exponential growth as straight line)
   - Explain nonrival nature of ideas
   - Comparative statics: scientist share, population, productivity
   - **Key lesson:** Ideas drive sustained growth, not capital

### Short-Run Macroeconomics (Final Emphasis)

6. **IS-MP-PC Framework**
   - Investment-Saving relationship
   - Monetary policy and interest rates
   - Phillips Curve inflation dynamics
   - Short-run vs. long-run effects

7. **Fiscal & Monetary Policy**
   - Government spending effects on GDP
   - Tax changes and crowding out
   - S - I = NX identity implications
   - Federal Reserve operations
   - Zero lower bound problem
   - Fisher equation: i = R + π

8. **Financial Crises**
   - Great Recession scenario analysis
   - Credit market disruptions
   - Policy responses

## Likely Exam / Practice Problem Types

### Type 1: Short Definitional/Conceptual (2-5 points each)

**Characteristics:**
- One or two sentences expected
- Tests basic definitions and understanding
- Common prompts: "Write a sentence describing...", "What exactly does X create?", "Briefly explain..."

**Examples from actual exams:**
- "What is capital in Econ 201?" (2 pts)
- "What does investment spending create?" (2 pts)
- "Roughly how big are C, I, G, NX as % of US GDP?" (2 pts)
- "Would you choose capital formation or idea production and why?" (3 pts)

### Type 2: Growth Rate Algebra (2-5 points each)

**Characteristics:**
- Apply one or more growth rate rules
- Show algebraic work
- Often involves taking growth rates of both sides of an equation
- May require Rule of 70 or compound growth formula

**Examples from actual exams:**
- "If g[M] = 5%, g[Y] = 3%, g[V] = 0, find inflation using MV = PY" (5 pts)
- "Take growth rate of both sides of Y^R = Y^N/P and derive relationship" (2 pts)
- "Compute growth rate of MPK given g[K] and g[L]" (typical PS question)

### Type 3: Solow Model Comparative Statics (20-35 points)

**Characteristics:**
- Multi-part question with narrative setup
- Part (a): Draw initial steady state diagram
- Part (b): Identify parameter that changes
- Part (c): Show short-run and long-run effects on graph
- Part (d): Explain transition dynamics
- Part (e): Interpret economic meaning

**Actual S26 Midterm Structure (21 points):**
- Scenario: US closes borders, no new immigration
- Draw Solow diagram with labeled axes, curves, steady state
- Identify which parameter changes (n decreases)
- Show how both curves shift
- Mark new steady state k** and y**
- Explain short-run vs. long-run effects on output per person
- Discuss recovery/transition dynamics

**Common scenarios from materials:**
- Immigration shock (L increases)
- Capital destruction (war, natural disaster)
- Saving rate change (policy)
- Productivity improvement (A increases)
- Zombie apocalypse (workers eaten → L falls)

### Type 4: Romer Model Analysis (15-25 points)

**Characteristics:**
- Derive growth rate of ideas
- Compute output path over time
- Graph on ratio scale
- Comparative statics on slope or level
- Explain nonrival nature of ideas

**Actual patterns:**
- Given z̄, ℓ, N, compute g[A]
- Write output at time t: y_t = ...
- Draw ratio-scale graph
- Show effect of: increased population, scientist share change, idea theft
- Explain why ideas matter more than capital for sustained growth

### Type 5: Short-Run Model Applications (20-40 points)

**Characteristics (from PS4 and final review):**
- IS-MP-PC diagram manipulation
- Policy shock analysis
- Multi-step reasoning about equilibrium changes

**Example structure:**
- Setup: economy in recession, Fed considers response
- Analyze: effect of interest rate cut on investment, output, inflation
- Graph: show shifts in IS, MP, or PC curves
- Interpret: short-run vs. medium-run outcomes

### Type 6: Numerical Calculations (5-10 points)

**Characteristics:**
- Plug numbers into formulas
- Solve for steady state values
- Compute specific growth rates or levels

**Examples:**
- "Given s=0.24, n=0.02, d=0.04, Ā=1, find k* and y*"
- "If GDP doubles in 20 years, what's the average growth rate?"
- "Country has y₀=10,000 and grows at 3%/year. Find y₂₀"

## Problem Generation Rules

### Rule 1: Match Professor Edwards' Style

**Distinctive features observed:**
- Whimsical warmup questions about pop culture, sports, campus life (always give full points)
- Real-world narrative framing (immigration, zombies, financial crises, war)
- Humor in question titles ("Get Shorty", "So how low can we go, Solow?")
- Clear point allocations for time management
- Explicit instructions: "Be specific and show your work"
- Often asks "Would this help or hurt X, or do nothing at all?"

### Rule 2: Parameter Change Identification

**For Solow/Romer problems:**
1. Present a real-world scenario
2. Student must identify which parameter changes (s, d, n, Ā, ℓ, N, z̄)
3. Student must determine direction of change
4. Student must trace through model implications

**Good scenario triggers:**
- Immigration → n or L changes
- Border closing → n decreases
- Capital destruction (war, disaster) → K falls but k* unchanged
- Innovation policy → z̄ or Ā increases
- Education investment → ℓ might increase
- Epidemic/disaster → L or N falls

### Rule 3: Graphical Requirements

**Solow diagram must include:**
- Properly labeled axes: k on x-axis, y on y-axis
- Production function: y = Āk^(1/3) curve
- Saving curve: sy curve
- Depreciation line: (n+d)k line (straight from origin)
- Initial steady state k* marked clearly
- New steady state k** if parameter changes
- Arrows showing transition dynamics if relevant

**Romer diagram must include:**
- Ratio scale on y-axis (log scale)
- Time on x-axis
- Straight line (exponential growth appears linear)
- Slope = g[A]
- Level changes vs. slope changes clearly distinguished

### Rule 4: Transition Dynamics Matter

**Always ask or expect discussion of:**
- Short-run effect (immediate impact)
- Long-run effect (new steady state)
- Transition path (how economy moves between them)
- Speed of convergence (further from k*, faster growth)

**Standard language:**
- "In the short run..."
- "In the long run..."
- "During the transition
